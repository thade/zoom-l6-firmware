# Writing in this repository

The docs are read by musicians, not reviewers. Write the way the
[README's "Using it" section](README.md#using-it) does: plain sentences,
one idea each, in the order the reader needs.

## Patterns to avoid

- A colon that sets up a punchline. "Nothing is copied: the pads play
  the file in place." Write two sentences, or join them with "because"
  or "so".
- A clipped fragment after a colon. "Both lights blink: on." Say what
  happens: "If both lights blink, the chain is now on."
- A reassurance tacked on the end. "...exactly as it always did",
  "...same as always", "the mixer remembers your choice". State the
  fact once, plainly, or drop it.
- A reveal. "...and the new take contains both: that is the overdub."
  Say what to do: "To overdub, play pad 1 while you record."
- "Simply", "just", "seamless", "robust", "powerful", "ensure". Cut them.
- Three short sentences in a row for rhythm. Vary the length because
  the content varies, not for effect.
- "Do X and you Y." Prefer "When X, Y" or "To Y, do X."
- Stating the same rule in two places so each reads complete. State
  it once where it belongs and let the other place refer to it.
- A heading with a trailing qualifier: "A stereo AUX send, switchable".
  Keep the noun; put the qualifier in the text.
- Bold on a phrase mid-sentence. Bold is for the lead-in of a
  paragraph or bullet, and for button names.

## Habits that help

- Name the thing the reader presses, sees or copies, in the mixer's
  own words: EFX, PAN, File Transfer Mode, L6.BIN.
- Give the fact, then the reason, then the exception, in that order.
- Say what was tested and what was not, with the date, and no more.
- Read the paragraph aloud. If a sentence sounds like a slogan, it is.
- Facts come from the code and the experiment READMEs, not from an
  earlier draft of the prose.
